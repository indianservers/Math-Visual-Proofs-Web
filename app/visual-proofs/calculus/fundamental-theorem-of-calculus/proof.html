Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

A small added area rectangle explains why \(A'(x) = f(x)\) for \(A(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt\).

Why it works

1
Small added area

Adding a thin rectangle from \(x\) to \(x + \Delta x\) adds area \(\Delta A \approx f(x)\Delta x\).

2
Average value becomes slope

As \(\Delta x \to 0\), the average rate of change \(\frac{\Delta A}{\Delta x}\) becomes the instantaneous tangent slope.

3
Equals the function value

The tangent slope at \(x\) equals \(f(x)\). Therefore, \(A'(x) = f(x)\).
