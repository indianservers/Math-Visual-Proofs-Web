Why it works

1 1. Slice
▼

A plane cuts both nappes of a double cone at a fixed tilt angle α and position height h. The orientation relative to the cone's semi-vertical angle φ determines the shape of the intersection curve.

e = \sin\alpha / \sin\phi
• α (Tilt angle): angle between cutting plane and horizontal base.
• φ (Semi-vertical angle): angle between cone axis and generator line.
• h (Height): position along axis (changes scale, not conic type).
2 2. Locus
▼

For any point P on the intersection, the ratio of its distance to focus F and perpendicular distance d to directrix ℓ is constant.

e = PF / d = \text{constant}
Current ratio: PF / d = 0.871
3 3. Conic type
▼

The value of eccentricity e determines the curve classification:

Circle (e = 0): Plane is horizontal (α = 0°).
Ellipse (0 < e < 1): Plane cuts one nappe (0° < α < φ).
Parabola (e = 1): Plane parallel to generator (α = φ).
Hyperbola (e > 1): Plane cuts both nappes (φ < α < 90°).
What happens at the borders?
▼

At the boundary angle α = φ (e = 1), the closed right oval of the ellipse stretches to infinity, transforming into an open parabola. When α exceeds φ, the plane cuts the upper nappe as well, creating the twin branches of a hyperbola!

Circle → Ellipse → Parabola → Hyperbola
