Why it works

1 1. Distances define the rule
▼
A conic is the set of points P such that the ratio between the distance from P to a fixed focus F and the perpendicular distance from P to a fixed directrix ℓ is constant.
PF / PD = e

Symbol Definitions:

P = point on the conic curve
F = fixed focus point
D = perpendicular foot from P to directrix ℓ
PD = perpendicular distance from P to directrix
PF = Euclidean distance from P to focus F
e = eccentricity constant

As you drag P along the curve, both individual lengths PF and PD change continuously. However, for every point belonging to the conic, their ratio remains constant and equal to e ($PF = e \cdot PD$).

2 2. e classifies the conic
▼

The magnitude of eccentricity e dictates how rapidly the point P moves away from focus F relative to directrix ℓ, uniquely determining the shape of the curve:

Circle (e = 0) e = 0

The circular limiting case where focus coincides with center and directrix is at infinity. Point P remains at a constant radius distance from focus.

Ellipse (0 < e < 1) 0 < e < 1

The focus-directrix distance ratio is strictly less than 1 ($PF < PD$). Because the focus pulls stronger than the directrix, the locus forms a closed oval curve ($\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$).

Parabola (e = 1) e = 1

Exact balance of distances ($PF = PD$). The curve breaks open into a single infinite parabolic branch ($y^2 = 4px$).

Hyperbola (e > 1) e > 1

Focus distance grows faster than directrix distance ($PF > PD$), creating two symmetric open branches ($\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$).

What happens at the borders?
▼

Understanding boundary transitions provides deep mathematical insight into why conic sections transition into one another:

  • e = 0 (Circle): Directrix is at infinity ($\ell \to \infty$). Distance to focus is constant ($PF = r$).
  • 0 < e < 1 (Ellipse): Focus pull dominates over directrix distance ($PF < PD$), keeping the locus bound within a closed ellipse.
  • e = 1 (Parabola Boundary): Critical threshold ($PF = PD$). The closed right side of the ellipse stretches to infinity, transforming into an open parabola!
  • e > 1 (Hyperbola): Directrix distance is overwhelmed ($PF > PD$), breaking the curve into two distinct open branches.
Circle → Ellipse → Parabola → Hyperbola
