Why it works

1 1. Equal Distances (Definition)
▼

By definition of a parabola, any point P on the curve is equidistant from the focus F and the directrix d.

Let us identify each key geometric object in the construction:
• P = any point lying on the parabola
• F = the fixed focus point
• d = the fixed directrix line
• D = the perpendicular foot from point P to the directrix line d

PF = PD

Here, PD represents the perpendicular distance measured straight across from point P to the directrix line d, forming a right angle at foot point D.

This geometric structure connects the focus, directrix, and point P into an isosceles configuration:

2 2. Equal Angles (Reflection Law)
▼

The tangent at point P makes equal angles with the segment PF and the axis of symmetry, so a horizontal ray reflects through the focus F.

Let us break down this reflection step-by-step:
1. An incoming light ray travels horizontally, completely parallel to the axis of symmetry.
2. The incoming ray strikes the parabola at the tangent point P.
3. The tangent line at P acts as a flat reflecting surface (mirror), determining the exact direction of reflection.
4. The angle of incidence ∠1 formed between the incoming ray and the tangent equals the angle of reflection ∠2 formed between the tangent and the reflected ray.
5. Because these relevant angles are equal, the reflected ray is directed precisely toward the focus F!

∠1 = ∠2

In our interactive visualization:
• ∠1 is the acute angle between the yellow incoming parallel ray and the pink tangent line.
• ∠2 is the acute angle between the pink tangent line and the purple reflected ray traveling toward focus F.

3 3. Hence
▼

Any ray parallel to the axis reflects through the focus.

This key optical theorem is established by following the logical chain of geometric dependencies:

1. Point P lies on the parabola.
2. By definition, PF = PD.
3. The tangent at P bisects the angle formed between PF and the perpendicular line from D, establishing the equal angle relationship.
4. The incoming ray is parallel to the axis of symmetry.
5. Because ∠1 = ∠2, the reflected ray must pass directly through focus F.
