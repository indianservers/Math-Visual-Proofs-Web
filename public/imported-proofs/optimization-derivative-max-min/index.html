Why it works
1
Tangent slope is \(f'(x)\).
The derivative \(f'(x)\) measures the instantaneous rate of change and slope of the tangent line to \(y = f(x)\) at point \((c, f(c))\).
2
Horizontal tangent \(\Rightarrow f'(c) = 0\).
At a local peak or valley, the curve transitions between increasing and decreasing. At this turning point, the tangent line is horizontal with slope \(0\).
3
Sign change in \(f'\) \(\Rightarrow\) extremum.
• + to −: Curve goes from rising to falling \(\Rightarrow\) Local Maximum.
• − to +: Curve goes from falling to rising \(\Rightarrow\) Local Minimum.
